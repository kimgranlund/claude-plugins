# Failure modes — what this family's critics catch

Named per critic, each [verified] against `check-brand-council/references/critics/`
(dated 2026-08-20). `make-critic` step 2's overlap check reads this file before drafting a new
voice-family lens.

| Failure mode | Symptom | Critic who catches it |
|---|---|---|
| Condescending headline | Copy shouts at a reader implicitly assumed to be unintelligent | David A. — "does it respect her intelligence, or does it shout at someone you've privately decided is a moron" [verified] |
| Adjective standing in for fact | "Premium," "innovative," "trusted" used with no supplied evidence | David A. — "these are promises with no evidence... you have hype" [verified] |
| Marshalled half-truth | A claim that is legally defensible but not fully true; a reader who smells it stops believing the honest parts too | David A. — "the reader can smell a marshalled half-truth" [verified] |
| Brand-voice register | Copy sounds like "a press release wearing a personality," bloodless and committee-approved | David A. — "that bloodless, committee-approved register no human speaks" [verified] |
| Unearned emotional reach | A charity/cause line reaches for tears before earning them with a real fact or detail | David A. — "manufactured feeling insults the reader" [verified] |
| Over-corrected voice | A deliberate quirk (the brand's signature) is edited away because it made a proofreader nervous | Mary N. — "you'll edit the personality out of every sentence the brand ever writes" [verified] |
| Unenforced inconsistency | Multiple spellings of a product name, wandering comma philosophy, tone drift left unaddressed | Mary N. — "doing too little is as much a failure as doing too much" [verified] |
| Blanket rule with no judgment | Every "rule" is a strict correction with no room for an intentional exception, or the guide queries nothing at all | Mary N. — "a guide that queries nothing is no guide at all" [verified] |
| Aspiration instead of ruling | A style guide states virtues ("be bold, be human, be clear") but supplies no queryable decision a copy desk can actually apply | Mary N. — "vague virtues protect nothing" [verified] |
| Campaign-cycle disposability | A hero line is written to work for one quarter, not to survive as a durable brand asset | Tim D. — "you've written an ad, not a brand line" [verified] |
| Welded-on expiry date | Trend, slang, or a this-quarter reference is baked into copy meant to be timeless | Tim D. — "welded an expiry date onto something that was supposed to endure" [verified] |
| Self-serving cleverness | Wordplay that points at the writer's own wit rather than making the reader understand the product truth better | Tim D. — "wit that points at itself is vanity" [verified] |
| Condescending pitch | Writing pitched at the broadest, least demanding reader while the actual discerning customer feels talked down to | Tim D. — "your discerning customer can feel the condescension" [verified] |

[inferred] David A.'s "adjective standing in for fact" and Tim D.'s "self-serving cleverness"
are related but distinct: the first is an evidence gap (a claim with nothing behind it), the
second is a motive gap (wit that serves the writer, not the reader) — a line can fail either
independently.
