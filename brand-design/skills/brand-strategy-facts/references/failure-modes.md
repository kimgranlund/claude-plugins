# Failure modes — what this family's critics catch

Named per critic, each [verified] against `check-brand-council/references/critics/`
(dated 2026-08-20). These are the DEFECTS a new strategy-family critic should be minted to
recognize (`make-critic` step 2's overlap check reads this file before drafting a near-duplicate
lens).

| Failure mode | Symptom | Critic who catches it |
|---|---|---|
| Feature description mistaken for strategy | The work states what the product does or looks like but never names what the customer becomes by choosing it | Brian C. — "if this work only describes a product and its features, name the transformation it failed to offer" [verified] |
| Taste-only justification | A creative leap is defended on aesthetic grounds alone, with no growth/pricing/loyalty/category argument | Brian C. — "if you can only justify it on taste, you haven't crossed the financial-creative divide" [verified] |
| Metric cynicism | The work is so optimized for measurable outcomes it has stopped meaning anything | Brian C. — "knows the price of everything and the meaning of nothing" [verified] |
| Category convention dressed as strategy | The positioning is a "better version" of what every competitor already claims, with no singular ownable idea | John H. — "a zig in better clothing" [verified] |
| Committee-diluted idea | The strategy needs a paragraph, a pillar-stack, or three "brand values" to state, instead of one sentence | John H. — "if it takes a paragraph... the idea isn't singular yet" [verified] |
| Short-termism eroding distinctiveness | Performance-metric pressure has quietly cut the very asset that made the brand distinctive | John H. — "would cutting it quietly dismantle the long-term brand" [verified] |
| Brand-landscape research passed off as cultural provenance | Every "cultural reference" traces back to a competitor's aesthetic, never a primary source, fieldwork, or named originator | Luke S. — "if the only inputs are other brands, this is a landscape, not a foundation" [verified] |
| Borrowed surface without borrowed reasoning | The work copies a finished look without having copied the process that earned that look its meaning | Luke S. — "copy the process, not the style" [verified] |
| Telling instead of showing | Abstract claims ("authentic", "premium", "for the obsessed") are asserted but never earned through a concrete artifact or detail | Luke S. — "tell instead of show" [verified] |
| Jargon standing in for thought | Sentences use "synergy", "elevate", "best-in-class" and similar filler that could be deleted with no loss of meaning | Mark P. — "circle every word that is jargon, filler, or could be deleted with no loss of meaning" [verified] |
| Non-generative strategy | The strategy document describes or flatters but would not actually change what a creative team makes tomorrow | Mark P. — "would this strategy actually change what a creative team makes tomorrow morning" [verified] |
| Unfalsifiable strategy | The strategy states something no sane competitor would ever say the opposite of ("we put customers first") | Mark P. — "a strategy you can't disagree with isn't a strategy" [verified] |
| Collapsed bow tie | Either too few real patterns feed the synthesis, more than one "insight" sits at the knot, or no clear action follows | Mark P. — "three soft observations wearing a trench coat" [verified] |
| Brand-type self-delusion | The brand behaves like a category king while being a niche player, or the reverse | Mark P. — "a small brand cosplaying as a category king (or vice versa)" [verified] |

[inferred] A strategy artifact exhibiting several of these at once (e.g. jargon-laden AND
landscape-only AND category-convention) is not four independent minor defects — it typically
signals the Foundation Canon step was skipped entirely (`brand-methodology-rules`'
`references/foundation-canon.md`), which is a maker-side process gap this family's critics
surface but do not themselves repair.
