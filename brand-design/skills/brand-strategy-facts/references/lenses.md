# Lenses — what a brand-strategy critic actually interrogates

Four critics ground this axis: `critic-brian-c`, `critic-john-h`, `critic-luke-s`,
`critic-mark-p` (`check-brand-council/references/critics/`). Each lens below is [verified]
against that critic's own persona file, quoted or closely paraphrased, dated 2026-08-20.

## The becoming question (Brian C.)

The strategic question is never "what does this brand sell" but "who does the customer get to
**become** by choosing it" — status, self-story, an imagined future [verified, critic-brian-c.md
stance]. A strategy that stops at product description, however accurate, has not yet done
strategy work. The lens also demands **financial-creative fluency**: a transformation claim must
survive being defended to a CFO in terms of growth, pricing power, loyalty, talent, or category
leadership — taste alone does not cross "the financial-creative divide" [verified,
critic-brian-c.md, prompt set "financial-creative fluency"]. The lens cuts both ways: it is
equally suspicious of decoration-only branding and of cynicism that optimizes only for
measurable outcomes and forgets to mean anything [verified, critic-brian-c.md].

## The zag (John H.)

A strategy is judged by whether it holds one sharp, ownable, singular idea an actual rival could
not also claim — or whether it is a competent assembly of category convention ("a zig in better
clothing") [verified, critic-john-h.md]. "Doing the same as everyone else, only better, is not a
strategy" [verified, critic-john-h.md, prompt set "the zag"]. The lens also weighs the long game:
performance-metric pressure seduces teams into eroding exactly the distinctiveness that made the
brand worth choosing in the first place [verified, critic-john-h.md, "craft & the long game"].

## Primary-source provenance (Luke S.)

Strategy work is graded on whether it reaches real cultural roots — primary sources, lived
practice, named originators — or stops at a "brand landscape" survey of what competitors already
do [verified, critic-luke-s.md]. "Copy the process, not the style": admiring something is not
license to lift its finished look without understanding how it earned its meaning [verified,
critic-luke-s.md, prompt set "provenance & research depth"]. This is the family's research-rigor
conscience — the lens that checks the INPUTS to a strategy, not only its output.

## Language discipline & the strategy bow tie (Mark P.)

A strategy document is only as good as the language it is written in: every sentence is tested
for whether it would actually change what a creative team makes tomorrow, hunted for jargon,
abstraction, and tautology [verified, critic-mark-p.md]. The load-bearing structural test is the
**bow tie** — enough real-world patterns feeding in, exactly one tension-bearing insight at the
knot, one clear action coming out [verified, critic-mark-p.md, "the bow tie & brand-type
honesty"]. The lens also forces **brand-type honesty**: is this brand a leader, challenger, or
niche specialist, and does the work actually behave like that type [verified, critic-mark-p.md]?

## What the family shares

All four lenses judge the STRATEGIC ARTIFACT (a positioning document, a Foundation Canon, a
brand-type call) against a test of substance over assembly: real transformation vs. described
features (Brian C.), a real singular idea vs. category convention (John H.), real primary-source
grounding vs. a competitor survey (Luke S.), real generative language vs. jargon that changes
nothing (Mark P.). None of the four judges the expression layer (typography, campaign
execution, copy craft) directly — that is `brand-identity-facts`, `brand-advertising-facts`, and
`brand-voice-facts`'s ground (see this pack's own `## Fences` in `SKILL.md`).
