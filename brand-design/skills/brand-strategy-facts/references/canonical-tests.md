# Canonical tests — the concrete diagnostics this family runs

Each test is [verified] as a specific, repeatable prompt drawn from a persona file
(`check-brand-council/references/critics/`, dated 2026-08-20) — the worked examples a new
strategy-family critic's calibration fixture (`make-critic` step 6) should be checked against
for genuine distinctness.

1. **The becoming test** (Brian C.) — ask who the customer gets to become by choosing this
   brand, not what they buy. If the honest answer is "nothing, they just get the product,"
   the strategy has not cleared the bar [verified].
2. **The CFO defense** (Brian C.) — restate the creative ambition's business case in terms a
   CFO would accept: growth, pricing power, loyalty, talent, category leadership. A defense
   that only invokes taste fails [verified].
3. **The one-sentence test** (John H.) — state the singular idea in one sentence. If it needs a
   paragraph, a pillar-stack, or three "brand values," the idea is not yet singular
   [verified].
4. **The rival-substitution test** (John H., overlapping with Luke S.'s "brand landscape" test
   below) — ask whether a rival could also claim this exact positioning. If yes, the work
   describes the category, not the brand [verified, paraphrased from "a better version of the
   obvious"].
5. **The provenance trace** (Luke S.) — trace every cultural reference in the work back to a
   primary source. A claim resting only on another brand's aesthetic, not on lived practice or
   a named originator, fails [verified].
6. **The receipts test** (Luke S.) — for any claimed subculture study, demand evidence of
   fieldwork, interviews, or primary-source archaeology, not a desk survey of the five obvious
   competitors [verified].
7. **The ruthless-edit test** (Luke S.) — strike every sentence, value, and visual that is
   generic enough to belong to a competitor; what survives is what the brand can actually stand
   on [verified].
8. **The jargon strike-through** (Mark P.) — circle every word that is jargon or filler and
   could be deleted with no loss of meaning; rewrite the worst sentence for a 14-year-old
   [verified].
9. **The generative-sentence test** (Mark P.) — for each strategy sentence, ask whether it
   would actually change tomorrow's creative output, or merely describes/flatters without
   provoking anything [verified].
10. **The falsifiability test** (Mark P.) — for each strategy claim, ask whether any sane
    competitor would say the opposite. A claim nobody would disagree with isn't a strategic
    choice [verified].
11. **The bow-tie test** (Mark P.) — verify enough real patterns feed the synthesis, exactly
    one tension-bearing insight sits at the knot, and one clear action follows [verified].
12. **The brand-type honesty test** (Mark P.) — name whether the brand is a leader, challenger,
    or niche player, and check the work actually behaves like that type [verified].

[inferred] Tests 3–4 (John H.) and 5–7 (Luke S.) target the same underlying failure —
convention/landscape masquerading as a real position — from two different entry points
(the strategy's OUTPUT vs. its INPUTS); running both surfaces a case one alone would miss when
a strategy quotes a genuinely obscure competitor as if it were a primary cultural source.
