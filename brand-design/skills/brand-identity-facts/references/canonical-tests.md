# Canonical tests — the concrete diagnostics this family runs

Each test is [verified] as a specific, repeatable prompt drawn from a persona file
(`check-brand-council/references/critics/`, dated 2026-08-20).

1. **The category-swap test** (Jessica W.) — cover the logo, read the strategy, and ask if
   three competitors' names could be substituted in with no loss of truth [verified].
2. **The focus-group-flinch test** (Jessica W.) — find the one element a focus group would
   flinch at; its absence means the work optimized for invisibility [verified].
3. **The AI-generation test** (Jessica W.) — ask whether a model could have produced this
   identity in thirty seconds; if so, name the missing human "wrongness" [verified].
4. **The three-level coherence test** (Massimo V.) — lay the semantic, syntactic, and
   pragmatic claims side by side and find which level fails first [verified].
5. **The grid test** (Massimo V.) — demand the actual modular grid be drawn, not a moodboard;
   its absence means there is no system, only a collection of pages [verified].
6. **The typeface-budget test** (Massimo V.) — name and defend every specified typeface; a
   half-dozen in a lifetime is the working ceiling, spent on one brand needs justification
   [verified].
7. **The fifty-year test** (Massimo V.) — strip the current trend and ask honestly whether the
   mark will read correct in fifty years or merely current today [verified].
8. **The proprietary-behavior test** (Matt W.) — defend renting a common typeface against
   drawing proprietary type behavior that makes a headline unmistakably the brand's own
   [verified].
9. **The body-text test** (Matt W.) — show the type system doing real work across a long
   feature, a caption, a pull-quote, and a legal line, not just three glamour headlines
   [verified].
10. **The issue-100 test** (Matt W.) — walk the system forward to its hundredth real
    application, made by people who aren't the original designer, and ask what has decayed
    [verified].
11. **The junior-designer handoff test** (Matt W.) — hand the system to a junior designer with
    a deadline and no access to the founder; ask whether it protects itself [verified].
12. **The liquidity stress test** (Paula S.) — apply the mark at a 16px favicon, on a building,
    in motion, in a foreign script, and on a hostile background; find where it breaks
    [verified].
13. **The color-and-name-stripped test** (Paula S.) — cover the wordmark and brand colors and
    ask whether anything unmistakably "this brand" remains in structure, type, or gesture
    [verified].
14. **The bold-gesture test** (Paula S.) — find the single decisive, slightly dangerous move;
    its absence means every choice was sanded down to be safe [verified].
15. **The editorial-refusal test** (Paula S.) — ask what the identity explicitly refuses to do
    (fonts, layouts, gestures); "anything goes" means no real discipline exists [verified].

[inferred] Tests 4–5 (Massimo V.) and 12 (Paula S.) are complementary, not redundant: the grid
test proves a system exists on paper; the liquidity stress test proves it survives real-world
deployment. A system can pass one and fail the other.
