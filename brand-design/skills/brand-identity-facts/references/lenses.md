# Lenses — what a brand-identity critic actually interrogates

Four critics ground this axis: `critic-jessica-w`, `critic-massimo-v`, `critic-matt-w`,
`critic-paula-s` (`check-brand-council/references/critics/`). Each lens below is [verified]
against that critic's own persona file, quoted or closely paraphrased, dated 2026-08-20.

## Find the weird, then the human signature (Jessica W.)

Identity work is judged first on specificity: could three competitors' names be swapped into the
same strategy and have it still read true? If so, the work "describes a category, not a brand"
[verified, critic-jessica-w.md]. The lens then moves to execution, demanding the **human
signature** — in the AI era, an identity a model could generate in thirty seconds is a failure
state; the work must show "the hand, the wrongness, the human decision a machine wouldn't make"
[verified, critic-jessica-w.md, "Human signature & self-knowledge"]. A third strand, "brand
therapy before identity," insists the excavation (what the brand is afraid of, what it secretly
believes) happens BEFORE typography and color are chosen, not after [verified,
critic-jessica-w.md].

## Discipline, timelessness, and three-level coherence (Massimo V.)

An identity system is read "as a logician reads a proof: one inconsistency invalidates the
whole" [verified, critic-massimo-v.md]. The lens tests coherence across three levels —
semantic (what the brand claims to mean), syntactic (the actual system: weights, colors,
grid), and pragmatic (how it behaves in use) — and demands they agree [verified,
critic-massimo-v.md, "Coherence across the three levels"]. It is explicitly hostile to
trend-chasing and decorative flourish, and tests every mark against a fifty-year horizon, not
today's fashion [verified, critic-massimo-v.md, "Timelessness and rigor"].

## Editorial typography as the lead element (Matt W.)

The lens treats the logo as "the least interesting part of an identity" and the type system as
the most, because a magazine's "recurring grammar" — not a single hero image — is what an
audience actually lives with over years of output [verified, critic-matt-w.md]. It specifically
tests whether type was drawn or merely rented, whether hierarchy survives long-form body copy
(not just glamour headlines), and whether the system can absorb content nobody has written yet
[verified, critic-matt-w.md, "Type as the lead element" and "The recurring-output test"].

## Liquid identity, scale, and editorial refusal (Paula S.)

Identity is judged "as a living system at scale," not a logo on a white page: does it flex
across a hundred contexts and sizes while staying unmistakably itself, or is it "a brittle,
single-lockup mark that breaks the moment it leaves the brand guidelines" [verified,
critic-paula-s.md]? The lens demands a bold, slightly dangerous gesture — a timid identity that
offends no one is forgotten by everyone — and insists a real identity is defined as much by
**editorial refusal**, what it deliberately won't do, as by what it does [verified,
critic-paula-s.md, "the bold gesture & editorial refusal"].

## What the family shares

All four lenses judge the VISUAL/SYSTEMIC IDENTITY artifact — marks, type, grid, color, the
system's behavior across contexts and years — never the strategic substance behind it (Jessica
W.'s "brand therapy" strand is the one bridge to strategy, explicitly framed as work that must
happen BEFORE identity, not identity work itself) and never copy/voice or campaign execution.
See this pack's `SKILL.md` `## Fences` for the sibling boundaries.
