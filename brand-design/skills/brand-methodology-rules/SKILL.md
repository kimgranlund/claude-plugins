# Brand Methodology

A brand is not a logo, a palette, or a personality deck. It is the **meaning a customer borrows to become someone**. This skill encodes how senior practitioners build that meaning: dig for cultural roots, decide a position, express it editorially, and steward it so it compounds.

## First principles

1. **Brand is a cultural mediator.** Never "what do we sell?" — **"who does the customer become by choosing us?"** Design the transformation, not the artifact.
2. **Cultural depth before expression.** Earn meaning from a real world — history, ritual, originators — before drawing anything. A brand assembled from a competitor moodboard is a _landscape_, not a foundation.
3. **Copy the process, not the style.** Study _how_ something earned its meaning and earn your own the same way; lifting a finished look imports the surface and none of the reason.
4. **Editorial over expressive.** Great brands behave like a great magazine — a strong POV, ruthless cuts, restraint as confidence. They omit more than they add.

## The pipeline

`RESEARCH → STRATEGY → EXPRESSION → STEWARDSHIP` — each stage gates the next. **No position before research. No expression before strategy.** Skipping a gate is the most common failure — it always shows up later as a brand that looks finished but means nothing.

| Stage | You produce | The gate it must pass |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Research** | Cultural archaeology: primary sources, fieldwork, the real subculture | Provenance — can every claim trace to a root, not a competitor? |
| **Strategy** | Positioning, point of view, the Brand Foundation Canon | Does it survive the bullshit filter (below)? Is there a trade-off? |
| **Expression** | Identity, voice/tone, the expression system | Is it downstream of a written strategy, or decoration? |
| **Stewardship** | Guidelines, governance, the rules of coherence | Can a stranger extend the brand correctly from the artifacts? |

## The Brand Foundation Canon (essence)

The foundation is the small set of **load-bearing** documents a brand actually stands on — everything expressive descends from them: six required components (cultural root, position, point of view, enemy/tension, customer transformation, editorial principles); a **bullshit filter** rejecting archetypes, vision/mission/values triplets, personas, and "brand DNA" word-clouds; and the **3-page minimum-viable foundation** test — unstatable in three pages means undecided.

## The three seats — aspire, make, review

Three distinct seats: the **Muse** supplies the _pull_ (an ideal, provocation, or guiding concept), the **Team** _makes_ (converges, the middle), the **Council** _reviews_ (judges against the aspiration, after) — **no seat judges its own work**. The Team is on-demand making roles (Strategic Planner, Creative Director, Copywriter, Art Director, Design Director, Product/UX Designer, Brand Steward). The loop is **aspire → make → review → remake**; aspiration is a **soft, precondition gate** — name at least a provisional pull, never skip it.

## Standing constraints

- **No convergence toward nothing.** An unnamed aspiration is a _soft_ gate — set a provisional, revisable pull first, don't stop making on it.
- **No values without trade-offs.** A value you would never sacrifice anything for is decoration.
- **Cultural research ≠ market research.** Archaeology maps the _culture_ (lived practice, primary sources, originators); market research only maps the landscape.
- **No expression before strategy.** No written foundation → stop and produce (or demand) it first.
- **The position must exclude.** A position that fits any competitor is not a position — name who it is _not_ for.
